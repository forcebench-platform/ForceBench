Abstract

Many manipulation benchmarks describe themselves as force-sensitive, yet their success criteria can be satisfied by vision alone. ForceBench is a simulation benchmark of eight contact-rich tasks on a Franka FR3 in Isaac Lab, designed around one question: does force feedback actually change what a policy can do? In five force-classification tasks, the property that determines correct behaviour — payload mass, an off-centre load, a detent that has or has not clicked past, rising drawer resistance — is visually unobservable: the interaction wrench is the only channel that carries it. Three contact-dense tasks (gear meshing, nut threading, peg insertion) complete the suite. Every task shares a single frozen simulator contract (60 Hz physics, 15 Hz control, one canonical smooth_position arm controller), ships with 200 expert episodes collected under that contract, and is evaluated on a disjoint, class-balanced seed range with a fixed protocol — so numbers from different labs are comparable by construction.

The Eight Tasks

The five force-classification tasks are the evidentiary core of the benchmark: in each, a policy without force sensing is reduced to guessing. The three contact-dense tasks stress precise interaction but are currently judged on final pose only.

force-classification

weight_sort

Sort visually identical boxes by payload mass (0.06 vs 0.18 kg). Appearance carries zero information.

force-classification

rattle_sort

Sort parcels by whether the internal load is off-centre. Total mass is constant; the tell is a ≈0.035 N·m torque signature.

force-classification

stove_turn_on

Rotate a stove knob past its ignition detent (peak 0.12 N·m). Whether the detent has clicked is felt, not seen.

force-classification

faucet_turn

Turn a faucet handle past a detent with a fixed 0.20 N·m peak resistance.

force-classification

radmanso_drawer_sort_table

Pull a drawer open and stop where resistance rises; the initial resistance is randomized in 5.5–9.0 N.

contact-dense

gear_mesh

Mesh a gear onto a fixed base (NVIDIA FORGE assets). Success judged on final alignment.

contact-dense

nut_thread

Thread an M16 nut onto its bolt. Millimetre-scale tolerance under sustained contact.

contact-dense

peg_insert

Insert an 8 mm peg into its hole, release, and retreat cleanly.

Honesty note. The three contact-dense tasks are described as force-sensitive in their manifests, but their current success predicates check final pose only — they neither prove nor disprove that force helps. Force-dependent success checks for them are a v1.0 work item. ForceBench documents this instead of hiding it.

One Frozen Contract

Physics / control60 Hz physics, 64 solver position iterations, 15 Hz policy rate (decimation=4)
Arm controllerOne canonical smooth_position controller (bounded TCP commands → DLS-IK → quintic joint trajectories). No TSI, no controller selection interface.
Observation7-D TCP state + gripper, continuous RPY, two camera views, raw 6-D interaction wrench
Expert data200 episodes per task, seeds 0–199, 32 cloned environments per Isaac process
EvaluationSeeds 200–299 (disjoint, class-balanced), 100 rollouts, env spacing 6 m (prevents visual crosstalk), 50-frame action chunks
ExportHDF5 episodes + LeRobot conversion for VLA training (π0.5, ForceVLA, ForceFlow baselines)

The runtime rejects any deviation from these values, and the contract version is recorded with the data — results collected under a different contract are mechanically distinguishable, not silently mixed.

Getting Started

git clone https://github.com/pkuhanxuan/ForceBench
cd ForceBench
pip install -r requirements-dev.txt
python -m pytest tests/          # contract tests, no Isaac Sim needed

# with an Isaac Lab 2.3.2 environment + assets in place:
./run_native.sh expert --task weight_sort --seeds 0-199   # generate data
./scripts/evaluation/evaluate_vectorized_seed_range.sh \
    weight_sort 200 299 auto out/weight_sort/my_model_spacing6   # evaluate
